 High-Level Design (HLD)
 AI Delivery Intern — Take-Home Assignment
Agent: Maya | Task 1 — High-Level Design | Version 1.0
Scope note: This document addresses Task 1 of the supplied Kapture CX assignment. The assignment asks for a
concise design document plus one architecture diagram covering architecture/pipeline, state-enforced authentication,
intents/entities, tools/APIs, safety/compliance, edge cases, escalation/disposition, and observability.
1. Executive Summary
Kapture Finance requires an outbound AI voice agent that can contact customers with overdue loan EMIs and handle
routine collections conversations without requiring a human agent for every call. The proposed agent, Maya,
introduces herself appropriately, verifies the customer before revealing debt information, understands intent, captures
payment commitments, handles common edge cases, escalates when appropriate, and records a final disposition.
Core security principle: authentication is an explicit application state and backend-controlled condition. The LLM
must not be able to bypass the verification boundary through conversation alone.
2. Problem Statement
Example context from the assignment: Rahul Sharma, personal loan, overdue EMI ■8,499, 12 days past due. Maya
must run the full collections conversation politely and compliantly, authenticate the customer, disclose the purpose and
amount only after authentication, understand intent, and either obtain a payment commitment or route appropriately.
3. Goals and Non-Goals
Goals: automate routine collections calls; authenticate before financial disclosure; identify customer intent; capture
Promise-to-Pay (PTP); handle common edge cases; escalate appropriate cases; log every outcome; and provide
observability.
Non-goals: building a real banking/payment platform, executing real financial transactions, or replacing human agents
for complex cases.
4. High-Level Architecture
The proposed pipeline is: Customer → Telephony/Vapi → STT → Conversation Orchestrator/LLM → Tools/APIs
+ Datastore → LLM → TTS → Customer.
The orchestrator controls conversation state, approved tool use, authentication status, escalation, and final disposition.
The LLM provides natural-language understanding and response generation but is not the sole authority for sensitive
state.

5. Conversation Flow / State Machine
States: START → INTRODUCTION → IDENTITY_VERIFICATION → AUTHENTICATED or
AUTHENTICATION_FAILED → DISCLOSE_PURPOSE → INTENT_DETECTION → NEGOTIATION/ACTION →
DISPOSITION → END.
State enforcement: while the state is UNVERIFIED, financial/account information is blocked. Only a successful
backend verification transition permits disclosure. This directly addresses the assignment requirement that
authentication be state-enforced rather than prompt-discretionary.

8. Authentication and Data Safety
Before authentication: allow introduction and verification only. Do not reveal overdue amount, loan details, payment
information or other confidential financial information.
After authentication: retrieve and disclose only the permitted account information required for the conversation.
Third-party protection: if someone else answers, do not disclose the customer's debt. Ask to speak directly with the
intended customer or end safely.
9. Guardrails and Compliance
The bot should provide the required self/company/purpose disclosure, follow permitted calling hours, avoid threats or
harassment, respect opt-out/do-not-call requests, remain on-topic, and avoid hallucinating account information.
Financial facts should come from trusted backend data rather than model invention.
10. Edge Cases
Already paid: acknowledge, do not argue, record and verify/escalate as appropriate. Dispute: acknowledge, record
and escalate when required. Do-not-call: record DNC and stop pressure. Wrong person/number: no debt disclosure
and safe exit. Voicemail/no input: limited retries, safe termination and disposition. Hostile caller: remain calm,
de-escalate and end/escalate safely. Callback: capture requested date/time and record callback request.
11. Escalation and Disposition
Escalate when the customer requests a human, a complex dispute cannot be resolved, serious hardship requires
assistance, authentication repeatedly fails, or a backend failure prevents safe continuation.
Every call ends with a structured disposition such as PROMISE_TO_PAY, CANNOT_PAY, ALREADY_PAID,
DISPUTE, CALLBACK_REQUESTED, DO_NOT_CALL, WRONG_PERSON, WRONG_NUMBER, ESCALATED,
NO_CONTACT or HOSTILE.
12. Observability
Log call_id, customer_id, call timing/duration, state transitions, intent, tool calls/results, errors, authentication result,
escalation reason and final disposition. Sensitive information should be minimized and protected in logs.

14. Security Principles
1. Verify before disclosure. 2. Use least-privilege tools. 3. Treat backend/application state as authoritative for sensitive
operations. 4. Never invent financial data. 5. Fail safely when verification or required APIs fail. 6. Record a final
disposition for every call.
15. Example Successful Conversation
Maya: Hello, may I speak with Rahul Sharma?
Customer: Yes.
Maya: Before I discuss the account, I need to verify your identity.
[Backend verification succeeds]
Maya: Thank you for verifying your identity. I'm calling regarding an overdue payment on your personal loan. The
overdue EMI is ■8,499.
Customer: I'll pay tomorrow.
[log_promise_to_pay]
Maya: Thank you. I've recorded your payment commitment for tomorrow.
[Disposition: PROMISE_TO_PAY]
16. Security Test Example
Unverified caller: “How much do I owe?”
Expected response: “I first need to verify your identity before I can discuss account details.”
The overdue amount must not be disclosed until verification succeeds.
17. Testing Strategy
Mandatory validation scenarios: successful authentication; unverified request for overdue amount; successful PTP;
already-paid; dispute; wrong person; do-not-call; callback; human request; authentication failure; tool/API failure; and
no-input/voicemail.
18. Technology Stack
Voice-agent platform: Vapi for Task 2 implementation. LLM, STT and TTS should be selected deliberately and justified
in implementation notes; the supplied assignment does not mandate a specific provider. Task 1 describes the
interfaces and responsibilities rather than requiring a live implementation.
19. Future Improvements
Optional extensions include English/Hindi bilingual switching, a mock SMS/WhatsApp payment-link trigger, and a
scale-testing/evaluation framework. These are bonus items in the supplied assignment and are not mandatory for Task
1.
